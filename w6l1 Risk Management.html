Introduction

  • Cybersecurity management = effective planning to safeguard digital assets; robust planning is the cornerstone of efficient asset protection.
  • Historically, planning has two lenses:

🚑 Contingency planning

  • Reactive: how you respond after incidents occur
  • Aim: mitigate impact on business processes
  • Without it: shocks, unpreparedness, big losses in revenue, reputation & continuity

🛡️ Preventive planning (incl. Risk Management)

  • Proactive: avert incidents altogether
  • Aim: protect organizational assets from threats
  • Needs a comprehensive inventory + valuation of all assets
💡
Key distinction: Contingency planning = resilience of business processes · Risk management = focus on the assets themselves.

The Philosophy: Know Yourself & Your Enemy

  • Roots in military strategist Sun Tzu: success needs two things.
😈
  • Understand the nature of external & internal threats in the environment.
  • Who might aim to cause harm or losses to the business?
🪞
  • Deep understanding of your own assets.
  • Your current preparedness + existing protection mechanisms against the threats.
  • Risk management = systematic process of identify threats ➜ assess vulnerabilities & assets ➜ reduce overall risk.
  • A continuous cycle, not a one-time event.
  • Complete elimination of risk is often impossible in the interconnected digital world.
2

Understanding Risk & Attack Surfaces

  • Risk = overall assessment of the chance a threat becomes an attack + the impact if it does.
  • Attack happens when a threat exploits a vulnerability ➜ incident.

The Concept of Residual Risk

  • Every org has some basic protection even without explicit cyber measures:
    • physical security (a security guard)
    • built-in OS safeguards (basic Windows protection)
  • = the "amount of asset value protected by safeguards".
  • Then additional safeguards: antivirus, firewalls.
  • Still vulnerabilities remain (eg: uninstalled updates/patches) ➜ fix them (regular patching) to reduce risk further.

Three Phases of Risk Management

🔍 Risk Identification

Understand "what you have" and "what can harm it". Detailed inventory of assets, threats, vulnerabilities.

⚖️ Risk Assessment

Evaluate: quantify impact & likelihood ➜ clear view of severity.

🎛️ Risk Control

Decide how to manage: select mitigation options, justify them, monitor effectiveness.

  • Distinct yet interconnected ➜ logical progression = comprehensive, systematic approach.
  • Ensures resources are allocated effectively to protect critical assets.

Phase 2: Risk Assessment (Value & Quantify)

  • Asset value = impact on the business, not merely purchase price.
  • Eg: e-commerce server ➜ compromise could halt all online operations, far exceeding hardware cost.
  • Done with expert input + questionnaires probing criticality:
⭐ Most critical to organizational success?💵 Generates most revenue / profitability?🛠️ Most expensive to replace or protect?⚖️ Highest liability if compromised?
  • Scored on a scale (eg: 1 to 5) + criteria can be weighted (revenue, profitability, public image) ➜ composite asset value score.
  • Eg: customer order system via SSL = score 100 (extremely critical).

📐 Two key measures

🔁 Loss Frequency

  • Probability/likelihood of a threat materializing & causing a loss
  • = inherent probability the threat occurs + likelihood it bypasses existing protections

💸 Loss Magnitude

  • Impact/cost if a threat successfully attacks an asset
  • Tied to asset value + financial, reputational, operational damage
🧮
Estimate both ➜ calculate residual risk quantitatively ➜ clear basis for understanding severity & informing risk control decisions.
7

Phase 3: Risk Control

  • Strategic decisions on how to manage the identified & assessed residual risks.
  • Not static: dynamic & ongoing ➜ constantly adapt to evolving threats & vulnerabilities.
