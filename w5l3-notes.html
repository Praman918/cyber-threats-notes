Digital Transformation & Growing Vulnerabilities

  • Driven by IoT, AI/ML, cloud ➜ faster, more efficient decisions ➜ indispensable for modern industry.
  • Companies handle terabytes of data daily ➜ data = critical asset.
  • But reliance ➜ significant fragility: transformation speeds up ➜ so do vulnerabilities.
  • Hardware/software so complex that even vendors may not understand all vulnerabilities.
  • Detecting threats: 200+ days on average, often only notified by third parties.
  • Why go digital? ↑ capability, ↓ human error, ↓ costs ➜ but ↑ cyber risk ➜ ↑ security costs.
  • Trade-off ⚖️ capability vs vulnerability ➜ needs proactive management, not just reaction.

Traditional Cyber Defense & Its Limits

  • "Cyber hygiene" ≈ personal hygiene for health ➜ protects data, users, networks.
🔍 Regular security checks⬆️ Software updates🎓 Employee training🧱 Network segmentation🔑 Complex password policies🩹 Periodic security patches🚪 Restricted access zones
  • Advanced performance monitoring scans IT environments, finds assets & vulnerabilities, gives risk scorecards.
  • Millions invested ≠ guaranteed protection against determined, targeted attacks.
  • Hackers constantly evolve: hard to escape sophisticated threats.
  • Full inventory of hardware & software is hard for asset-intensive industries (transport, energy).
  • Large dispersed systems (eg: power stations with many substations) ➜ upgrades & breach containment incredibly complex.
  • ⚠️ Single entry point for a hacker ➜ could collapse the entire grid.
  • So investing more in current tech can't entirely prevent attacks ➜ need a different strategy.
🧠
Hygiene = brushing your teeth 🪥: stops common problems, but won't save you from a determined, resourced attacker.

A New Approach: CCE & CIE

  • Andrew Bochman: senior grid strategist, Idaho National Laboratory (INL).
  • Idea rooted in "cyber insecurity": no digitally connected system is truly secure.
  • Core aim: reduce dependence on digital platforms & internet for critical functions.
  • INL built two frameworks: CCE and its companion CIE.

💎 CCE Consequence Driven Cyber-Informed Eng.

  • Higher, strategic level
  • Change how leadership sees threats: not a "security problem" to solve, but an "insecurity" to manage
  • Goal: build redundancy so critical functions keep running
  • For national-security-level events or well-resourced, state-sponsored actors

📐 CIE Cyber-Informed Engineering

  • Design stage: cyber risk considered from the conceptual phase, esp. industrial digital platforms
  • Not securing after it's built
  • Saves time, ↑ security, redundancy from the outset; impact understood across the whole product lifecycle
  • Handles cyber-physical attacks & targeted information-system attacks (more granular)
🧠
Both: don't abandon automation ➜ complement it with built-in redundancies & contingency plans. CCE = Chief-level (strategy) · CIE = Engineer-level (design)

4 steps to protect the most vital assets (click through):

  • Think like an adversary: war-game scenarios to find attack vectors.
  • Strong cyber safety culture: staff recognize early signs (small glitches or trips) before they become critical failures.
  • Contingency planning is paramount: redundant systems must NOT also be connected to the internet ➜ no simultaneous compromise.
