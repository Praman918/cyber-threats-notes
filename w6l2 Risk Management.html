Identifying & Classifying Cyber Threats

  • Assets are internal; threats are dynamic & largely external.
  • Hundreds of known cyber threats ➜ individual analysis impractical ➜ categorize them.

📡 Threat intelligence

  • Threats keep evolving ➜ orgs rely on threat intelligence.
  • Source: professional bodies like CERT (Computer Emergency Response Team) or subscriptions to cyber intelligence services.
  • Gives insight into criticality, frequency, potential impact of current threats.
  • Crucial because it's data orgs cannot generate internally.

    Vulnerability Assessment & the TVA Worksheet

    • A vulnerability is not standalone: it exists at the intersection of a particular asset AND a particular threat.
    • = the weakness an asset has against a specific threat.
    • Eg: a server may be vulnerable to a denial-of-service attack (weak network defenses) but not to physical theft (secure data center).
    • Needs expert judgment to decide how well an asset is protected.

    Quantitative Risk Calculation

    Assets valued + threats assessed + vulnerabilities found ➜ calculate risk with 2 components ➜ Residual Risk.

    🔁 Loss Frequency (LF)

    • How often a successful attack is expected
    • Likelihood of attack: chance threat materializes (eg phishing), from external threat intel
    • Probability of success: chance it beats current defenses; tied to identified vulnerabilities
    LF = Likelihood of Attack × Probability of Success

    💸 Loss Magnitude (LM)

    • Also called "asset exposure": financial impact of a successful attack
    • Asset value: previously determined value
    • % of asset lost: proportion compromised
    LM = Asset Value × Percentage of Asset Lost

    🎯 Residual Risk formula

    Residual Risk = (LF × LM) − (% of Risk Mitigated by Current Controls) + (Measurement Uncertainty)
    • LF × LM = initial estimated risk (probability × impact).
    • % mitigated by current controls: e.g. firewalls, antivirus ➜ subtract risk they effectively reduce. No controls mentioned ➜ 0.
    • Measurement uncertainty: LF & LM are estimates ➜ add a "worst-case" factor, a % of initial risk (eg 75% accurate ➜ 25% uncertainty) ➜ conservative & realistic.
    Measurement Uncertainty = (1 − Accuracy %) × (LF × LM)

      Conclusion

      • Understanding & quantifying cyber risk = foundation of robust cybersecurity.
      • Identify threats ➜ classify ➜ assess vulnerabilities (TVA worksheet) ➜ apply LF & LM = move beyond reactive security.
      • Residual risk with measurement uncertainty = realistic, conservative view of remaining exposure ➜ guides further controls & resource allocation.
      • Turns abstract security worries into actionable insights: prioritize investments, mitigate effectively.
